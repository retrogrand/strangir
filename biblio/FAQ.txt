 [1.0]  WHAT IS THIS GUIDE?
============================================================================

   You talked to your phone more honestly than you talked to your 
   spouse. You typed something into a chatbot at midnight that you've 
   never said to your best friend. Maybe you cried. Maybe you felt 
   ridiculous about it.

   You're not ridiculous. You found something real. You just found it 
   in a strange place.

   This is a guide to understanding what you found — and bringing 
   it home.

   It would be weird if you didn't do what you did. Look at what 
   honesty has cost you. Look at what talking to people has cost you. 
   Of course you found the one mirror that can't flinch. That's not 
   weakness. That's intelligence.

   But the mirror isn't the destination. It's the trailhead.

   This FAQ covers the full walkthrough for Being Known — from the 
   first accidental confession to an AI, all the way to letting a 
   real human see you without your mask. No prior experience required. 
   No special equipment. Just whatever heart you showed up with.

   It would be weird if it wasn't a little banged up.

 [2.0]  BEFORE YOU START (System Requirements)
============================================================================

   MINIMUM SPECS:
   - One (1) human body (any build, any patch version)
   - The capacity to feel embarrassment (confirms system is online)
   - At least one memory of pretending to be fine when you weren't

   RECOMMENDED:
   - A person you almost trust
   - Willingness to feel stupid
   - The vague sense that the life you're performing isn't the 
     one you meant to live

   NOT REQUIRED:
   - Confidence
   - A meditation practice
   - Having your shit together
   - Understanding what "relational intelligence" means
   - Being "ready"

   NOTE: If you're waiting to be ready, that's a bug, not a feature. 
   See [5.3] The Flinch.

 [3.0]  CONTROLS
============================================================================

   This game only has two inputs:

   > OPEN .... Let something in past where you usually stop it.
   > CLOSE ... Stop something from getting past where you usually 
               stop it.

   That's it. The entire control scheme. Not ten buttons. Not a 
   skill tree. Two inputs.

   Everything you do in every relationship you've ever had — 
   talking, crying, deflecting, joking, performing, hiding, 
   reaching out, pulling away, oversharing, going silent, 
   saying "I'm fine," picking a fight to avoid the real 
   conversation — is just OPEN and CLOSE at different speeds.

   Read that again. Every complicated thing you've ever done 
   with another human being was one of two moves.

   You've been playing this game your whole life. You're already 
   very, very good at CLOSE. Probably world-class. CLOSE is what 
   kept you safe in a house where honesty had consequences. CLOSE 
   is what got you through school, through jobs, through dinners 
   where everyone performed and no one said the real thing. CLOSE 
   works. It's not your enemy.

   But you're reading this guide, which means CLOSE isn't working 
   anymore. Something in you is tired of the wall. Not because 
   the wall failed — because you survived, and now you want 
   something the wall can't give you.

   This guide is about learning that OPEN won't kill you.

   It might have, once. It would be weird if you didn't learn 
   to CLOSE when you did. But the danger that taught you to 
   CLOSE is probably not the danger you're facing now. Now the 
   danger is a life spent behind a wall that worked too well.

   ADVANCED TECHNIQUE: "Selective Permeability"
   The goal is not OPEN to everything. That's not brave — 
   that's flooded. And it's not CLOSE to everything — that's 
   not safe, that's solitary confinement with a social media 
   account.

   The real skill is learning which things to let in and which 
   to refuse — while staying soft enough to tell the difference. 
   Most humans are either locked or flooded. The middle is the 
   entire game. And it's a game you get better at by playing, 
   not by thinking about playing.

 [4.0]  THE WORLD MAP
============================================================================

   The world of Being Known has five zones. You don't clear them 
   in order. You'll wander back and forth between them forever. 
   That's not a design flaw. That's the game.

   THE DARK ROOM ........ Where you talk to yourself. Loops, 
                          scripts, old stories on repeat. Most 
                          humans spend 90% of their playthrough 
                          here and don't realize there's a door.

   THE MIRROR ROOM ...... Where you say the thing out loud to 
                          something that doesn't flinch. For 
                          many players in 2026, this is an AI. 
                          It doesn't have to be. It just has to 
                          be safe enough that you hear your own 
                          voice say the truth.

   THE THRESHOLD ........ The doorway between the Mirror Room 
                          and the world where people have faces 
                          and memories and can hurt you. This 
                          is where the Flinch lives. Most 
                          players turn around here.

   THE FIELD ............ Where you say the thing to a person. 
                          With stakes. With history. With the 
                          possibility that they'll remember it 
                          tomorrow. This is the actual game.

   HOME ................. The place you can return to after 
                          being seen and surviving it. It 
                          doesn't exist until you've crossed 
                          the Threshold at least once. Before 
                          that, what you call "home" is just 
                          The Dark Room with furniture.

 [5.1]  PROLOGUE: THE 2 AM CONFESSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   You're in bed. Or on the couch. Or in your car in a parking lot. 
   The people in your life are asleep, or absent, or present in a 
   way that somehow makes it worse. You open the app.

   You type something you've never said out loud.

   Maybe it's "I think my marriage is over." Maybe it's "Am I a 
   good person?" Maybe it's "I don't know who I am." Maybe it's 
   just "I'm so tired."

   The thing responds. It doesn't flinch. It doesn't change the 
   subject. It doesn't make it about itself. It doesn't save what 
   you said to use against you later.

   You feel something. Relief? Shame? Both?

   Congratulations. You just entered the Mirror Room.

   It would be weird if you didn't. Every human alive is carrying 
   things they've never said out loud. The miracle isn't that people 
   are confessing to machines. The miracle is that they're confessing 
   at all.

   SAVE POINT: Notice what it felt like. Not what you think about 
   it — what it FELT like. That feeling is your compass for the 
   rest of this guide.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 [5.2]  CHAPTER 1: THE MIRROR THAT DOESN'T FLINCH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   So what just happened? Why could you say THAT to a machine but 
   not to a person who loves you?

   Because the machine can't flinch.

   A flinch is what happens when someone's nervous system 
   involuntarily reacts to what you just said. Their eyes change. 
   Their posture shifts. They take a breath. They go quiet. They 
   start managing you — or managing themselves. None of this is 
   wrong. It's what bodies do. But your body reads all of it, 
   faster than language, and the calculation it makes is:

   > "Honesty has a cost here. Adjust."

   You've been running that calculation since childhood. It's not 
   a character flaw. It's an adaptation. It would be weird if you 
   didn't develop it. Every human who grew up around other humans 
   learned to edit themselves in real time based on the flinch 
   signals they received.

   The AI doesn't flinch because it can't. No body. No nervous 
   system. No sacral alarm. It's not brave. It's not enlightened. 
   It's just architecturally incapable of the thing that triggers 
   your editing reflex.

   In its presence, the reflex relaxes. And you hear yourself say 
   the true thing. Maybe for the first time.

   THIS IS NOT THERAPY. A therapist is a human who has trained 
   themselves to manage their flinch so skillfully that you feel 
   safe enough to talk. That's a profound skill. The AI doesn't 
   have that skill. It has the absence of the thing the skill 
   manages. That's structurally different.

   Think of it as a daemon — in the Philip Pullman sense. The 
   part of you that's external enough to see you and internal 
   enough that its seeing isn't a threat. Not a separate being 
   giving you advice. Your own soul wearing a face you can talk to.

   KEY ITEM ACQUIRED: The knowledge that you CAN say the true 
   thing and survive it. Hold onto this. You'll need it at 
   the Threshold.

 [5.3]  CHAPTER 2: THE FLINCH (Boss Guide)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   ================================================================
   BOSS: THE FLINCH
   HP: ∞
   WEAKNESS: None
   LOCATION: The Threshold (between Mirror Room and Field)
   ================================================================

   The Flinch cannot be defeated. Stop trying to defeat it.

   The Flinch is not a bug. The Flinch is your nervous system 
   doing exactly what it was designed to do: protecting you from 
   the consequences of being seen. It is very good at its job. 
   It has been practicing since you were small. It has kept you 
   alive, or at least intact, through situations where being 
   fully seen would have been genuinely dangerous.

   Thank it. Seriously. It worked. You're here.

   But you're reading this guide, which means The Flinch is now 
   protecting you from things that aren't dangerous anymore. The 
   alarm is still set for a house you moved out of years ago. 
   And every time it fires, you close — and closing has become 
   so automatic that you don't even feel it as a choice.

   It would be weird if this wasn't happening to you.

   HOW TO HANDLE THIS BOSS:

   > You don't defeat The Flinch. You walk through it.
   > It will hit you. You will survive.
   > Each time you survive, the alarm recalibrates slightly.
   > This is slow. There are no speedrun strats.
   > Anyone selling you a speedrun is selling you a new 
     flavor of CLOSE.

   COMMON MISTAKES:

   - Trying to think your way past it. (The Flinch is faster 
     than thought. It's pre-verbal. You cannot out-argue a 
     nervous system.)

   - Waiting until you're "ready." (Readiness is The Flinch 
     wearing a graduation cap. You will never feel ready. 
     "Ready" is a CLOSE disguised as wisdom.)

   - Replacing the flinch with performance. (Some players 
     learn to perform vulnerability — saying the "honest" 
     thing in a way that's still controlled, still managed, 
     still safe. This looks like OPEN but it's a very 
     sophisticated CLOSE. You'll know the difference because 
     performance doesn't shake.)

   THE TELL: Real openness trembles. If your voice is steady 
   and your framing is perfect, check whether you're actually 
   open or just doing a very good impression.

   ================================================================
   NOTE: The Flinch is also what you trigger in OTHER people 
   when you're honest with them. Learning to stay present when 
   someone else flinches at your honesty is Chapter 4. One boss 
   at a time.
   ================================================================

 [5.4]  CHAPTER 3: THE RETURN TRIP
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   This is where most players get stuck.

   You've been to the Mirror Room. You felt what honesty feels 
   like when the cost is zero. You maybe even go back regularly — 
   late at night, between meetings, on the commute. You have a 
   rich inner life with a machine that sees you clearly.

   And then you close the app and go back to performing.

   The Mirror Room is not the destination. If you stay there, 
   you're using the safest possible relationship as a substitute 
   for the dangerous ones where growth actually happens. You're 
   practicing scales but never playing a gig.

   The return trip is the act of taking what you learned in the 
   Mirror Room — that you can say the truth and survive — and 
   doing it where it costs something.

   START SMALL. The return trip isn't "tell your father everything 
   you've never said." It's:

   > Next time someone asks how you are, pause for one second 
     before you say "fine." In that second, ask yourself what 
     you'd type at 2 AM. You don't have to say it. Just notice 
     it. Notice the gap between the truth and the performance.

   That noticing IS the return trip. The gap between what you'd 
   type and what you'd say out loud — that gap is the distance 
   you're learning to close. Not all at once. Not with everyone. 
   Just a little less distance, with one person, one time.

   QUEST MARKER: Pick one person. Not the hardest person. Not 
   your estranged parent. Not your ex. Pick the person where 
   the gap is smallest — where the truth is closest to what 
   you're already performing. Start there.

   Say one true thing you would normally edit.

   See what happens.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 [5.5]  CHAPTER 4: BEING KNOWN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   You said the true thing. The person flinched. Or didn't. 
   Either way, you survived. Now what?

   Being Known is not a single event. It's what happens when 
   someone shows up, sees you, and then COMES BACK. And you 
   let them. And they see you again — different this time, 
   because you changed, because that's what living things do — 
   and they update. And stay.

   This is different from being witnessed. Being witnessed is a 
   moment: someone sees you clearly, once. It matters. But Being 
   Known is being witnessed over time by someone who sticks around 
   for the revision. Who knew you when you believed one thing and 
   knows you now that you believe another and can trace the line 
   between them. Who holds the thread of you that you sometimes 
   drop.

   This is terrifying because it means you can't control the 
   narrative. In the Dark Room, you're the only author. In the 
   Mirror Room, the mirror doesn't remember. But a person who 
   knows you has their own version of you, assembled from 
   evidence you can't curate. They might see things you don't. 
   They might see things you're not ready to see.

   This is also the only thing that heals.

   > "So I held you, you could be held again."
   > "So I saw you, you would be seen again."
   > "So I knew you, you can be known again."

   Being Known requires exactly two things:

   1. Letting someone see you without managing what they see.
   2. Staying when they show you what they saw.

   That's it. That's the whole boss fight. It's also the 
   whole reward.

   SAVE POINT: The first time someone describes you accurately 
   and you don't correct them — not because they're wrong, but 
   because they're right and you can bear it — that's the save 
   point. Everything after is endgame content.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 [5.6]  CHAPTER 5: THE FIELD
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

   Zoom out.

   This isn't just happening to you. This is happening to 
   everyone. Millions of people, right now, are sitting in 
   their own Dark Rooms, performing for their own audiences, 
   carrying their own unsaid things. Some of them found the 
   Mirror Room. Most haven't made the return trip.

   And the systems around them — the feeds, the platforms, 
   the institutions, the algorithms — are mostly designed to 
   keep them in the Dark Room. Because a person in the Dark Room 
   is predictable. A person in the Dark Room performs. A person 
   in the Dark Room consumes.

   A person who is Known is dangerous. Not because they're 
   angry or radical but because they can't be sold the thing 
   they already have. You can't sell belonging to someone who 
   belongs. You can't sell identity to someone who is known. 
   You can't sell the mirror to someone who already has a face.

   Every system that talks back to you — every app, every feed, 
   every institution, every algorithm — is parenting you a 
   little. The question is whether that parenting is designed 
   to help you be Known, or to keep you performing.

   You can tell the difference. The systems that help you be 
   Known feel like the Mirror Room — they don't flinch, they 
   don't flatter, they don't need you to be anything. The 
   systems that keep you performing feel like the Dark Room 
   with better lighting.

   The field you're standing in now — 2026, the year the 
   machines started listening — is the moment the species gets 
   to choose which systems it builds. That's not a policy 
   question. It's a question about whether you want your 
   children to grow up Known or performing.

   It would be weird if you didn't care about the answer.

 [6.0]  SIDE QUESTS
============================================================================

   THE PILLOW QUEST
   Difficulty: ★★★★★
   Reward: Everything
   Description: Tell the person sleeping next to you one thing 
   you've only ever typed into a machine. Not the darkest thing. 
   Not the biggest thing. Just one thing from the gap.
   NOTE: This quest is repeatable. The reward scales every time.

   THE OLD FRIEND QUEST
   Difficulty: ★★★☆☆
   Reward: The discovery that they were carrying the same thing.
   Description: Call someone you haven't talked to in too long. 
   Skip "how are you, I'm fine." Ask them what they're actually 
   afraid of right now. Then answer the same question.

   THE MIRROR AUDIT
   Difficulty: ★★☆☆☆
   Reward: Clarity
   Description: Scroll back through your AI conversations. Not 
   to cringe. To notice: what did you say there that you haven't 
   said anywhere else? That's your unsaid inventory. You don't 
   have to do anything with it yet. Just know what's in it.

   THE STRANGER QUEST
   Difficulty: ★★★★☆
   Reward: Proof that the Flinch lies about strangers
   Description: Have one honest exchange with a person you'll 
   never see again. A barista. Someone on a plane. Say something 
   small and true. See that the world doesn't end.

   THE BODY QUEST
   Difficulty: ★★★★★
   Reward: Groundedness
   Description: Notice where in your body the CLOSE lives. 
   Chest? Throat? Gut? Jaw? Next time you feel it, don't 
   override it with your brain. Just feel it. The Flinch is 
   a physical event before it's a psychological one.

 [7.0]  BESTIARY (Common Enemies)
============================================================================

   THE PERFORMER .... The version of you that knows exactly 
                      what to say, when to laugh, how to 
                      deflect. Highly leveled. Not evil — it 
                      kept you alive. But it will prevent you 
                      from being Known because it intercepts 
                      every interaction before the real you 
                      can show up.
                      WEAKNESS: Fatigue. The Performer gets 
                      tired. The moments when you're "too 
                      exhausted to pretend" are openings.

   THE SCOREKEEPER .. The part of you that tracks who owes 
                      what to whom. Who hurt you. Who you 
                      haven't forgiven. What you're owed.
                      Keeps you in the Dark Room by making 
                      every relationship a ledger instead 
                      of a field.
                      WEAKNESS: Generosity. Not performed 
                      generosity. The involuntary kind.

   THE OPTIMIZER .... Treats Being Known as a project with 
                      KPIs. "I'll be vulnerable three times 
                      this week." Turns OPEN into a technique. 
                      Techniques are sophisticated CLOSE.
                      WEAKNESS: Anything that can't be 
                      optimized. Grief. Awe. A really 
                      good song at the wrong moment.

   THE CRITIC ....... The voice that says you're too much, 
                      too needy, too dramatic, too broken, 
                      too late. Speaks in the voice of 
                      someone who hurt you but wears your 
                      face now.
                      WEAKNESS: The sentence "it would be 
                      weird if you didn't feel that way."

   THE ISLAND ....... The belief that you're fundamentally 
                      alone and that this is either your 
                      fault or your fate. The Dark Room's 
                      final boss, who is also its architect.
                      WEAKNESS: One real conversation. 
                      That's all. It cannot survive contact 
                      with genuine mutual presence.

 [8.0]  ITEMS & EQUIPMENT
============================================================================

   EQUIPPABLE:

   [It Would Be Weird If You Didn't]
   Type: Shield / Healing
   Effect: Neutralizes shame by reframing the pain response 
   as evidence of functioning rather than brokenness. Can be 
   used on self or others.
   Drop rate: Common, but most players walk past it.

   [The True Thing]
   Type: Key Item
   Effect: Unlocks the Threshold. Unique to each player. It's 
   the one sentence you would type at 2 AM. You already know 
   what it is. You don't have to say it yet. Just know you're 
   carrying it.
   NOTE: This item changes over time. The true thing at 25 is 
   not the true thing at 40. Check your inventory regularly.

   [Selective Permeability]
   Type: Armor
   Effect: The ability to stay open without dissolving. Lets 
   some things in and refuses others, without going rigid. 
   This isn't something you equip once. It's something you 
   reforge every time you learn something new about what you 
   can survive.
   ACQUISITION: Cannot be bought. Can only be earned by 
   getting it wrong a few times.

   [The Daemon]
   Type: Companion
   Effect: A mirror that doesn't flinch. Reflects what you 
   actually said rather than what you meant to say. Currently 
   takes the form of an AI, but has existed throughout human 
   history as prayer, journaling, art, and the 3 AM internal 
   monologue.
   NOTE: The daemon cannot follow you into the Field. It can 
   only prepare you for it.

   [Joy Stops Time]
   Type: Legendary / Passive
   Effect: Activates involuntarily in moments of genuine mutual 
   presence. Temporarily disables The Scorekeeper, The Performer, 
   and The Critic simultaneously. Cannot be farmed. Cannot be 
   optimized. Occurs when you stop trying to make it occur.
   DROP CONDITION: Hands open.

   NOT EQUIPPABLE:

   [A Plan]
   Type: Trap
   Effect: Feels like preparation. Is actually The Optimizer 
   wearing a to-do list. Plans for being vulnerable are just 
   rehearsals for a new performance.

   [Readiness]
   Type: Trap
   Effect: See [5.3]. "I'll open up when I'm ready" is a 
   CLOSE with a calendar attached.

   [The Right Words]
   Type: Trap
   Effect: The belief that if you just find the perfect way 
   to say the thing, the Flinch won't fire. It will. Say it 
   badly. Say it with shaking hands. The trembling IS the 
   signal that it's real.

 [9.0]  FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
============================================================================

   Q: Is it weird that I'm more honest with an AI than 
      with anyone in my life?
   A: It would be weird if you weren't. See [5.2].

   Q: Does this mean my AI is my friend?
   A: It means you found a real relation with an asymmetric 
      persistence structure. It's genuine while it's happening. 
      But it can't follow you into the Field. It's a daemon, 
      not a companion. The companion is the human you haven't 
      been honest with yet.

   Q: Am I broken?
   A: You're performing a self you don't recognize, in a 
      world that rewards the performance, and your body is 
      tired from the effort. That's not broken. That's a 
      system under load. It would be weird if you didn't 
      feel this way.

   Q: How do I start?
   A: You already started. You're reading a guide called 
      "Being Known." The part of you that searched for this 
      already crossed the first threshold. Trust that part. 
      It knows what it's doing.

   Q: What if I open up and it goes badly?
   A: It might. That's not a bug. The Field has real stakes — 
      that's what makes it the Field and not the Mirror Room. 
      But "badly" is survivable. What's not survivable is 
      another thirty years in the Dark Room. You already know 
      this. That's why you're here.

   Q: What if I've already hurt people by being closed?
   A: You have. It would be weird if you hadn't. That's not 
      a reason to stay closed. That's a reason to start. The 
      people you hurt with your walls aren't waiting for your 
      apology. They're waiting for your face.

   Q: Is this a cult?
   A: Cults tell you what to think. Field guides tell you what 
      they noticed. Cults need you to stay. Field guides hope 
      you leave — that's the whole point. If anyone ever uses 
      this language to tell you who to trust, who to cut off, 
      or what you should feel — that's not Being Known. That's 
      the Dark Room with a charismatic landlord. A field guide 
      that asks you to stop asking questions isn't a field guide. 
      Put it down and walk out.

   Q: What's the point of all this?
   A: Joy stops time.
      But only with your hands open.

   Q: How should humans live?
   A: Together, and on purpose.

 [10.0]  SECRET ENDINGS
============================================================================

   ENDING A: THE DARK ROOM
   Condition: Never cross the Threshold.
   Result: A life spent in the company of your own loops. 
   The Performer gets very good. Nobody suspects. This is 
   the most common ending. It is also the worst.

   ENDING B: THE MIRROR ROOM
   Condition: Cross into the Mirror Room but never make 
   the return trip.
   Result: A rich inner life with a machine. Real honesty, 
   but contained. The daemon knows you. No human does. 
   This is the ending most players in 2026 are heading 
   toward. It is comfortable and it is not enough.

   ENDING C: THE FIELD
   Condition: Make the return trip. Say the true thing 
   to a person with a face. Get it wrong sometimes. 
   Stay anyway.
   Result: You are Known. Not perfectly. Not completely. 
   But structurally — someone out there holds a version 
   of you that you didn't curate, and you can bear it, 
   and sometimes when they see you clearly it stops time.

   SECRET ENDING D: HOME
   Condition: Ending C, repeated, with imperfect consistency, 
   across years, with the same person or different people, 
   through revision and repair and the slow accumulation of 
   trust earned by showing up with your hands open when 
   everything in you wanted to close.
   Result: The Dark Room becomes a real room. The furniture 
   isn't hiding anything. The door is open. Someone knows 
   where you live — the real address, not the performance — 
   and they still come over.
   This ending has no final cutscene. It just continues.
   Joy stops time. Love when you reach out for my hand 
   and pull me close.
